The Conviction Game

You don't just pick horses. You say how sure you are. Then the truth tells you who was right.

~10 min read · 30 apr 2026 · plain english

Every Saturday, thousands of Australians sit down with a tip sheet, a beer, and an opinion. They study the form. They argue with their mates. They develop strong reads on which horses are going to win. Then they bet — alone — into a system designed to take their money.

Their collective intelligence is enormous. It's also completely wasted. The bookmakers absorb it through odds movement and keep the spread. The punters generate the signal; the bookies keep the value.

The Conviction Game is what happens when that signal is captured, measured, and returned to the people who made it.

01 · The problem

Why nothing tracks who's actually good.

You probably know someone in your group whose tips are weirdly reliable. The mate who called Black Caviar three weeks running. The uncle who picked the wet-track winner that nobody else saw. You'd trust their pick over a stranger's any day.

But here's the problem: there's no way to prove it.

Right now, when you tell a punting forum or a tipping app "I like Horse 5 in Race 3," the system records it as a binary. You picked it or you didn't. There's no measure of how sure you were. There's no track of how often you've been right before. There's no weight to your opinion based on your record.

So a few things happen, all of them bad:

Tipsters who write the loudest

Get the most followers. Whether or not their actual strike rate is any good. The market rewards confidence, not accuracy.

The crowd's wisdom evaporates

Every Saturday's collective intelligence — pub conversations, WhatsApp groups, trackside reads — is generated and lost. Nobody captures it. Nobody learns from it.

Bookies absorb the signal

They watch where the money goes, adjust the odds, keep the spread. Three operators — Sportsbet, Ladbrokes, TAB — control most of a $32 billion market on this dynamic.

Punters can't compound their edge

If you're sharp, that sharpness stays trapped in your head. You can't build a reputation that travels. You can't help your mates without writing a column.

The structural problem is that punting platforms treat all picks as equal. They don't ask how sure you are. They don't track who's right over time. They don't aggregate honest opinions into something that pushes back on the bookmaker's spread.

The Conviction Game does all three.

02 · What it is

A weekly game where conviction is the currency.

Each week, a few hundred punters log in (or open WhatsApp, or check their app). They see the meetings, the races, the fields. They see the consensus picks the group has formed so far. And they see something nobody else's platform shows them: a budget of conviction credits.

You don't pick horses. You allocate conviction across them. You can put a lot of conviction on the horse you're certain about, a little on the horse you're leaning toward, and nothing on the races where you have no read.

a saturday morning, in plain words

"I'm dead-set on Hillier in Race 4 — that's worth 5 conviction points. Race 3 I lean Cantiamo, maybe 2 points. Races 1, 6, 7 I've got nothing today, no allocation. Race 8 I think the favourite's overlooked, 3 points on Black Babylon."

That's it. That's the game. Once everyone has allocated, the system aggregates — heavier conviction counts for more. The races run. The results come in. The system records who was right and who was wrong, and crucially, how confident they were when they said it.

Over weeks and months, the data accumulates. It becomes possible to say, mathematically: this person has a 38% strike rate when they put 4 or more conviction points on a horse. That person has a 62% strike rate but only on wet tracks. That person is consistently better than the consensus on Saturdays, worse on Wednesdays.

Reputation stops being a feeling. It becomes a number. And a sharp punter's number keeps getting better the longer they play.

03 · How it works

Three small ideas that fit together.

1 · Conviction has a price, and the price grows fast.

You don't get unlimited conviction. You get a weekly budget — say, 100 credits. And the cost of conviction grows the more you use:

conviction level credits it costs in plain words
1 1 "I'm leaning that way."
2 4 "I think this is the one."
3 9 "I'm pretty confident."
4 16 "I'd put my reputation on this."
5 25 "I am dead-set certain."

Doubling your conviction costs four times as much. Tripling it costs nine times. This isn't punishment — it's forced honesty. You can't say "everything is my best pick." Your budget literally won't allow it. You have to choose where you actually have an edge.

The technical name for this is quadratic voting — a mechanism economists have been studying for years. The result: when 100 people each spend 1 credit on their honest reads, they collectively produce 3.3 times more signal than if one person dumps 100 credits on a single horse. Distributed honesty beats concentrated bluffing, mathematically.

2 · Truth arrives every thirty minutes.

Most prediction games have a problem: you can't tell who was right. Was that political analyst correct? Debated for months. Did that economic forecast hold up? Argued for decades.

Horse racing doesn't have that problem.

Race 4 at Randwick starts at 14:30. By 14:32, we know who won. There is no committee. No interpretation. No appeal. The horse crossed the line first. That's the truth.

Australia runs roughly 200 races a week. That's 200 truth events. Every conviction allocation gets graded against an objective answer within 30 minutes of the race starting. You don't get to argue about whether you were right. The track tells you.

This is what makes the game work. Without ground truth, conviction allocation is just opinion. With ground truth, it's measurable skill.

3 · Reputation compounds.

Each week you play, the system records how your conviction allocations performed. Over time, this builds a track record:

  • How often you're right when you say "1 point" of conviction.
  • How often you're right when you say "5 points" — your dead-set picks.
  • Whether your accuracy is consistent or streaky.
  • Whether you're better at certain meetings, distances, track conditions.

And here's the magic: as your record gets stronger, your conviction starts carrying more weight in the group's aggregate. The system doesn't treat the brand-new player the same as someone with a six-month verified track record. The aggregate reflects who's actually been right.

This means: if you're sharp, your influence grows. If you're not, you stay a small voice. And critically, nobody can buy their way to influence. There's no premium tier where you get bonus conviction. Conviction is earned, one race at a time, against truth.

04 · What it feels like

Saturday morning, but the chat group is measured.

Imagine a Saturday in six months. You wake up, make a coffee, open the app. The week's racing is laid out — eight meetings across the country, fifty-odd races between them.

You see your conviction budget for the week: 100 credits. You see the consensus that's formed already — heat-mapped horses, weighted by who allocated. You see your own track record: "You're 43% on conviction-3+ picks at metropolitan meetings." You see your friends' allocations, your group's aggregate.

You read the form. You watch a couple of trial replays. You make your calls.

One race in, one of your 5-conviction picks wins. Your phone buzzes — your group's chat group lights up. Your reputation ticks up a fraction of a percent. The aggregate re-weights for next week.

Three races later, your 4-conviction pick on a longshot in Race 7 doesn't run a place. Reputation ticks down. You'll be more conservative on longshots next week.

By the end of the day, the leaderboard has moved a little. You're up two places. The punter who's been on a hot streak in your group has dropped one — his conviction was pointing the wrong way today.

what you actually feel

For the first time, the punters around you can see who's been right. Not because they're loud about it. Because the math says so. You're playing for credibility, not money. And credibility you've earned travels.

And here's the kicker: it's free. There's no bookie taking a cut. There's no money on the horses (you can still bet privately if you want, but the game doesn't require it). The game is the thing. The credibility is the prize.

05 · Why now

Three things changed at the same time.

The data flow is automatic.

Until recently, getting structured tip data out of newspapers, magazines, and TV broadcasts was a manual, brittle process. AI vision models can now read a tip sheet image, identify the publication, the meeting, every tipster, every selection — and explain what it did. The data layer underneath the game runs itself.

The audience is already gathered.

Australian punters already cluster — pubs, WhatsApp groups, racing forums, paid tipping services. We don't need to build a community from zero. We need to give existing communities a better instrument. The first 50 players come from one existing customer base. The next 500 come from the SEO long-tail of "who's actually good at picking horses."

The infrastructure is mature.

Quadratic voting, anonymous reputation, real-time aggregation — these were research problems five years ago. Today they're production patterns with mature open-source tooling. The mechanism that makes this game fair is no longer the bottleneck. The bottleneck is whether someone has the audience and the architectural taste to ship it.

The honest summary: this isn't a moonshot. It's a known mechanism applied to the right wedge — horse racing — at a moment when the data layer finally works. The first version requires no on-chain anything, no token, no novel cryptography. Just a clean read of what punters already do, structured into a game where being right is finally measurable.

06 · The close

One sentence.

The Conviction Game is a free weekly horse-racing prediction game where players don't just pick horses, they say how sure they are — and over time the system learns whose conviction to trust, building the first verifiable picture of who in Australia is actually good at picking horses, owned by the punters who built it instead of the bookmakers who currently extract from them.

That's the whole thing. Everything else — the technology, the tiers, the Matrix community, the eventual API — is just the architecture beneath the sentence.

Saturday morning, you wake up, make a coffee, open the app. You make your calls. Truth arrives every thirty minutes. The aggregate gets sharper. So do you.

appendix

For people who want the next layer down.

Everything above is the player's experience. Underneath, there's a deliberate architecture that makes the game fair and the data valuable. In short:

concern how it's handled
How do tips get into the system? AI vision agent reads tip sheets from any source — newspapers, magazines, broadcasts. Same tech that solved Pete's "xx prefix" bug.
How are race results captured? Automated scraper hits every Australian meeting nightly, settles all conviction allocations against ground truth.
Where does the data live? Convex — a real-time database with type-safe queries. Every player's allocation history, every tipster's track record, every race's settled outcome. Hosted in Australia.
Can the game be gamed? Quadratic-cost conviction makes large coordinated allocations exponentially expensive. Anonymous-reputation primitives (later phases) prevent identity-based bots. Honest play is the cheapest play.
How do new players catch up? Their conviction starts at base weight. Their record is built race by race. Within a month of regular play, a sharp new player's reputation reflects their actual skill — not their seniority.
What's the business model? Free WhatsApp tier as onramp. Subscription Matrix tier ($25/mo) for community features and full conviction game. Premium API tier ($100+/mo) for cross-meeting intelligence consumed by syndicates and media.
Is this gambling? No. No money on horses. No payouts based on race outcomes. Conviction credits are non-monetary, non-transferable, reset weekly. Players can still bet privately with their bookmaker — the game is independent.

Note on the Conviction Game label: longer-form versions of this concept include zero-knowledge proofs, anti-collusion primitives, and on-chain reputation. Those are future phases (months 12+). The version a punter plays in month one is the simple version above: weekly budget, quadratic cost, ground truth, compounding reputation. Everything else accrues as the system earns it.
